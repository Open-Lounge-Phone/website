Every Open Lounge Phone is the same small open circuit board inside whatever body suits the room.
The board, its firmware and every case are code in the repository, so you can build one, change
one or design your own.

<Aside type="caution" title="Where the hardware stands">
  Four phones on the first dev boards ([hardware
  v0.1](https://github.com/Open-Lounge-Phone/open-lounge-phone/releases/tag/hw-v0.1)) are built
  and working, lightly tested. Board v0.2 is designed but not built. The cases are designs,
  checked against the board in CAD. To start, use the
  phone in a browser; we'll recommend building your own at board v1.0.
</Aside>

## The board

- An ESP32-S3 with Wi-Fi
- 12 swappable mechanical keys and a hook switch, and a small e-paper status display
- a 3.5 mm jack for an off-the-shelf classic G-style handset
- a piezo ringer, one status light, and USB-C for power and updates (no battery)

**No microphone on the board.** The only microphone is in the handset: unplug the handset and
there is no microphone connected at all, and while it is hung up the software keeps it muted.
The phone is powered like a landline, from the wall: not a mobile device.

### Board v0.2: Ethernet and PoE (in design)

The next revision adds an **RJ45 jack**, so the phone can run on a network cable instead of
Wi-Fi, and **PoE**: one cable for network and power, like an office desk phone. USB-C still works,
and either one powers the phone. It adds a WIZnet W5500 Ethernet chip, a jack with built-in
magnetics, and an isolated 802.3af PoE module. It's still one board with no I/O expander: the
Ethernet chip shares the display's wires.

It's designed and checked (schematic checks, power budget, full layout with zero design-rule
errors, case fit), but **not built or tested yet**. The phone firmware already drives the
Ethernet chip: with a cable it uses the cable and turns Wi-Fi off, without one it uses Wi-Fi
(tested in the simulator so far). Parts come to about $18 a board.

**Power:** one Ethernet cable from a PoE switch port or a PoE injector (IEEE 802.3af or 802.3at)
runs the whole phone, up to 100 m. Or use a USB-C charger. A computer's USB port is for flashing
and the console only, with the PoE cable plugged in too. "Passive" 24 V PoE injectors won't power
it.

## Build it yourself

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="About $8 in parts per board" icon="setting">
    The chips, connectors and passives on board v0.1 come to about $8.50 (about $6 in volume).
    You hand-solder only two parts: the handset jack and the display socket.
  </Card>
  <Card title="About $23 in parts per phone" icon="rocket">
    Board parts plus switches, keycaps, a 2.13" e-paper module and an antenna, with a budget
    handset (estimates). Board fabrication, assembly and shipping are extra and depend on your
    order.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>
