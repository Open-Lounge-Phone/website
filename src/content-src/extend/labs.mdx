The labs firmware is the phone firmware plus a set of experiments. It's built separately from
the phone's own firmware (the 0.9 line), so the phone stays as it is, and you can switch between
the two over USB at any time: your settings are kept.

The experiments that proved themselves are in the phone firmware now: the
[house intercom, the chime and the daily check-in](/how-to/using-the-phone/#home-features),
turned on per phone in the app. Labs builds on them.

<Aside type="caution" title="Status: tested on a computer, not yet on a board">
  The settings, the intercom's logic (room to room, all rooms, busy, doorbell, chime) and the
  check-in are tested on a computer, and the firmware runs on the board (its screens and settings
  page). The intercom between two boards, Home Assistant, the USB keyboard and stories still need
  trying on hardware.
</Aside>

## What it does

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="House intercom" icon="random">
    A key rings another room: lift, press "Kid's room", talk. "All" rings every room and the
    first to lift answers. Phones talk straight to each other on your Wi-Fi: no server, no
    internet. Uses the phone's own intercom, so turn it on for the phone in the app.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Doorbell or front desk" icon="approve-check-circle">
    A phone by the door: its key (or, in doorbell mode, any key) rings the phones inside with the
    door's name. Whoever lifts talks to whoever is at the door.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Dinner's ready" icon="star">
    One key and every phone on the intercom beeps and shows the words.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Daily check-in" icon="heart">
    Reminders such as `08:30 PILLS` show until a key is pressed, and a key can be "I'm OK" for
    the phone's daily check-in (its time is set in the app).
  </Card>
  <Card title="Home panel" icon="setting">
    A key calls a Home Assistant service (lights off, a scene), and the idle screen can show a
    status such as `GARAGE: OPEN`, refreshed every minute.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Dial-a-story" icon="open-book">
    A key plays a recorded story in the handset from any web server on your network. 1 plays it
    again, 2 the next one, BACK stops.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Macro pad" icon="laptop">
    The phone becomes a USB keyboard: keys send shortcuts, mute, play/pause or volume. Twelve
    mechanical keys and a hook switch on your desk.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Games" icon="puzzle">
    Press MENU ten times: 1 Snake on the e-paper screen (3, 8, 7 and 9 steer), 2 Memory (watch the
    digits, key them back). BACK leaves.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## Set it up

Build and flash it from the repository (ESP-IDF 5.5, see the [firmware reference](/reference/firmware/)):

```sh
firmware/labs/labs.sh build     # build
firmware/labs/labs.sh flash     # build and flash the board on USB
```

Then on the phone press **MENU**, then **4 LABS** (or **3**, then **2**, when 4 is the phone's
intercom or chime): it shows the phone's address (for example
`OLP-KITCHEN.LOCAL`) and a PIN. Open that address in a browser on the same Wi-Fi, enter the PIN,
and set the room name, the mode, what each key does, Home Assistant, the reminders and the
doorbell. Keys you leave as "phone" stay the server's speed-dial keys.

To go back to the regular phone firmware, flash it from `firmware/` the usual way.

## Good to know

- The intercom, doorbell and chime work on your own home network, among phones of the same
  household with the intercom on: their messages are signed and their audio is encrypted with
  the household's intercom key, which the phones get from their server once.
- The Home Assistant token is write-only: the settings page never shows it again.
- In macro-pad mode the USB port is a keyboard, so the USB console is off and flashing may need
  the BOOT button held.

Every setting, the key actions and the intercom's messages on the wire are in the
[labs firmware reference](/reference/labs-firmware/). Ideas for where these fit:
[shared houses](/uses/shared-houses/), [grandparents](/uses/grandparents/),
[front desks](/uses/front-desks/), [makers](/uses/makers/).
