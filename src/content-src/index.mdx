## Why it exists

<div class="why">

There was a time when calling someone meant a landline or a payphone. Then phones moved into our
pockets and the landline faded: who wants to pay for one more phone to check?

Something went with it. A phone that lives in one place. Calling a home, not a pocket. Knowing
that whoever picks up is there because they want to talk, not because a buzz interrupted them.

Open Lounge Phone brings that back on purpose: real keys, a handset that stays put, and it rings
only for the people you've said yes to, answered at your leisure.

**Your calls, on your terms.** We live in an age where you can run your own communications. It
works over any internet connection: your own server, a friend's, or the free public hub, all
talking to each other as equals. It even runs with no internet at all, on a server inside your
home, school or building, so calls never leave the premises ([run it
offline](/how-to/run-it-offline/)). Nobody owns the network but the people on it.

</div>

## One board, many phones

The same small open ESP32 board, in whatever body suits the room. Board v0.1 runs on Wi-Fi.
Board v0.901 adds Ethernet and PoE, so one network cable runs it (designed, not built yet).

cases](/hardware/boards-and-cases/) has the details, files and costs (about $23 in parts per
phone).

## How it works

Everything runs on **connections**. Nobody reaches you until you've accepted their knock, and
there's no phone number to spam and no bridge to the phone network, ever. You choose who can
reach you, when you're taking calls, and whether your connections see that you're around.

<Architecture />

- **Calls go straight between the two people**: the audio never passes through the server.
- **Voicemail everywhere**, with a greeting and a transcript.
- **Rooms and party lines**, 3-way calls, hold and transfer.
- **Lounge phones** anyone takes over as themselves, and that forget them afterwards.
- **Passkeys**, no passwords or email; your own address, `name@server`.
- **Any old phone or tablet** can be the phone: open `/device/` and add it to the home screen.
- **Your server or ours:** your own Cloudflare account, a Node server with SQLite, or the free hub.

<p class="licence-line">

<Aside type="note" title="Where things stand">
  **The software works today, end to end:** run your own server or join the hub, pair a phone,
  make real calls, set quiet hours, get voicemail with transcripts. Until the hardware ships, the
  phone runs in a browser: an old tablet or laptop on a shelf makes a fine first phone. **The
  hardware is an early prototype:** four phones on board v0.1 are built and working, lightly
  tested. Start with the phone in a browser; we'll recommend building your own at board v1.0.
</Aside>

## A free public hub

Social media is dead, but being social isn't. The hub gives you an address your friends and
family can reach you on, with you deciding who gets through and when. You don't need the
hardware: the phone runs in any browser too.

The hub is **free and funded by donations**. Most calls go straight between the two people, so it
costs little to run. [Sign up at hub.openloungephone.app](https://hub.openloungephone.app).
