## What works today

- **Calls** between phones and the grown-ups' companion app, peer to peer (audio never passes
  through the server).
- **Default-deny contacts:** a guardian decides exactly who can call the phone and who it can call.
- **Quiet hours** per household, with a clear "quiet until 7:00" on the phone.
- **Voicemail everywhere:** a call that isn't answered — no answer, declined, busy, quiet hours —
  goes to a greeting (the standard one, your recorded name, or your own), and the message lands
  in the right inbox, with a transcript.
- **Accounts, passkeys and invites:** your own address (`name@server`), households, teams and
  organizations; add family by link; sign in with a passkey, no passwords or email.
- **Connections across servers ("knock, then talk"):** knock on someone's address on any Open
  Lounge Phone server; once they accept you can call, and a guardian can let them call a kid's
  phone. Blocking, limits and signed server-to-server requests keep it spam-free. No phone
  numbers and no bridge to the phone network, ever.
- **Rooms and 3-way calls:** family party lines and phone rooms with their own address
  (`standup@your.server`) that anyone allowed can drop into — from the app or a phone's key; hold a
  call, add a caller and merge; transfer a call.
- **Lounge phones:** a shared phone anyone takes over as themselves — scan its code, press the key
  it flashes — even people from other servers, and it forgets them afterwards.
- **Any device as a phone:** open `/device/` on an old phone or tablet and add it to the home
  screen.
- **Two ways to run it:** your own Cloudflare account (Workers, Durable Objects, D1, R2) or a
  self-hosted Node server with SQLite.

## The hardware (v0.1 early dev board prototype)

One small, deliberately minimal open circuit board built around an ESP32-S3:
- 12 swappable mechanical keys and a hook switch, and a small e-paper status display
- a 3.5 mm jack for an off-the-shelf classic G-style handset
- a piezo ringer, one status light, and USB-C for power and updates (no battery)

**No microphone on the board.** The only microphone is in the handset: unplug the handset and
there is no microphone connected at all, and while it is hung up the software keeps it muted.
The phone is mains/USB-powered like a landline — no battery by design, not a mobile device.

It sits in a simple 3D-printable base, and you pick the colour, keycaps and cord. Schematic,
board layout and a prototype box are all code in the repository, so anyone can build or modify one. Follow
along in [Build the hardware](/how-to/build-the-hardware/). The first boards,
[hardware v0.1](https://github.com/Open-Lounge-Phone/open-lounge-phone/releases/tag/hw-v0.1) (rev A), were ordered on 2026-09-30: that release has the exact Gerbers,
parts lists, schematic and a build guide.

### Build it yourself

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="About $8 in parts per board" icon="setting">
    The chips, connectors and passives on the board come to about $8.50 (about $6 in volume). You
    hand-solder only two parts: the handset jack and the display socket.
  </Card>
  <Card title="About $23 in parts per phone" icon="rocket">
    Board parts plus switches, keycaps, a 2.9" e-paper module and an antenna, with a budget
    handset (estimates, 2026-09-30). Board fabrication, assembly and shipping are extra and
    depend on your order.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

Files: [hardware v0.1 release](https://github.com/Open-Lounge-Phone/open-lounge-phone/releases/tag/hw-v0.1)
(Gerbers, parts list, build guide) · [hardware folder](https://github.com/Open-Lounge-Phone/open-lounge-phone/tree/main/hardware)
(schematic, KiCad layout, box).
