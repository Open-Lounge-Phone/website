A shared house has a kitchen, a living room and a front door, and people who come and go. A few
phones peppered through it make it easier to reach each other without a group chat.

## What it does

- **A house phone anyone can use.** Put a [Lounge phone](/how-to/lounge-phone/) in the kitchen.
  Anyone in the house scans its code, presses the key it flashes, and it's their phone: their
  people on the keys, their calls ringing there. When they walk away it forgets them.
- **Who's around.** Press MENU, then 5 to show you're open to chat; idle phones elsewhere in the
  house can show who's here (off until the house turns it on).
- **House-line keys.** An idle Lounge phone can have keys that call as the house: one housemate,
  someone's desk phone, or several housemates at once, where the first to answer takes it.
- **A party line.** An always-open [room](/how-to/rooms-and-3-way-calls/) everyone in the house
  can drop into.

## Around the house (labs)

With the [labs firmware](/extend/labs/), phones on the same Wi-Fi talk to each other directly,
with no server and no internet:

- **Intercom:** lift, press "Kitchen", and the kitchen phone rings. "All" rings every room.
- **Doorbell:** a phone by the front door rings the house phones with the door's name; whoever
  lifts talks to the door.
- **Dinner's ready:** one key, every phone beeps and shows the words.

The labs features are tested on a computer but haven't run on a real board yet. The intercom
is meant for your own home network: its audio isn't encrypted (calls through a server are).

## Start

1. [Get started](/getting-started/) and create the house as a space.
2. [Set up a Lounge phone](/how-to/lounge-phone/) on an old tablet in the kitchen.
3. Add a party line under [Rooms](/how-to/rooms-and-3-way-calls/).
