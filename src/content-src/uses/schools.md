A classroom needs a way to reach the office that isn't a teacher's own mobile, and the office
needs to reach a classroom without a walk down the hall. A phone on the classroom wall or desk
does both, with nothing on it that a class could wander into.

## What it does

- **The office in one press.** Each classroom phone has the office staff, the nurse or the
  caretaker on its keys, and reaches any ring group by its extension (MENU, 6, the number). The
  office rings a classroom by its extension too.
- **Ring groups for the staff.** "Office" rings whoever is on duty at the front desk, all at once
  or in turn, and goes to the office's shared voicemail if nobody answers.
- **School hours.** Outside the hours you set, calls go to voicemail or to the person on call.
- **Calls to a partner class.** A room with its own address lets two classes, even at schools on
  different servers, talk on a call the teachers set up: a pen-pal class, a visiting author, a
  sister school abroad.
- **Every call stays in the school's space.** The school's admins see the call history and the
  changes made; nobody outside reaches a classroom unless the school has connected with them.

The classroom phone belongs to the school, not to a child. The kids' phone rules (a guardian's
allow-list, quiet hours) are for home phones; at school the staff decide who the classroom
phones reach.

## Start

1. [Get started](/getting-started/) with an **Organization** space for the school.
2. [Pair a phone](/how-to/pair-a-phone/) in each classroom, and set up
   [extensions and ring groups](/how-to/workplace-phone-system/).
3. For class-to-class calls, make a [room](/how-to/rooms-and-3-way-calls/) and put it on a key.

A classroom phone can be an old tablet today, running the phone in a browser. The hardware is an
early prototype: see [Boards and cases](/hardware/boards-and-cases/).
